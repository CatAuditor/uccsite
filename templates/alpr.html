Surveillance Investigation

Ten cameras in one Utah county were searched 5.1 million times by police in 45 states

Weber County, Utah operates ten license plate reader cameras. Between February 2022 and July 2026, records the county released show 3,343 law enforcement agencies ran 5,171,087 searches against the networks it administers.

Weber County's own deputies ran 13,100 of them.

Download the complete dataset

Every figure on this page comes from records Weber County released under Utah's Government Records Access and Management Act, or from documents the county published itself.

Utah Civic Compact Active Records: February 2022 – July 2026  ·  110 files  ·  5,171,087 search records

We would like to thank the Weber County Sheriff's Office for providing these records and maintaining a culture of transparency throughout this process.

Nothing on this page is a criticism of that office, its personnel, or their integrity. Weber County did not design this platform, did not write its sharing defaults, and did not create the gap in Utah law that leaves no agency able to verify how others use the data it shares. Every finding here should be assumed to describe the situation at agencies across the state. The difference is that Weber County is the only one that has let anyone look.

What Utah Law Requires

Utah is not a state without rules here. It has had an Automatic License Plate Reader System Act since 2013, amended in 2023. This is what it says.

The default is that you may not use these systems at all

"Except as provided in Subsection (2), a governmental entity may not use an automatic license plate reader system."

— Utah Code § 41-6a-2003(1)

There are four exceptions, and only four

"an automatic license plate reader system may be used... by a law enforcement agency to access captured license plate data:
(i) as part of an active criminal investigation;
(ii) to apprehend an individual with an outstanding warrant;
(iii) to locate a missing or endangered person; or
(iv) to locate a stolen vehicle"

— § 41-6a-2003(2)(a)

The first exception is not open-ended. The statute defines it:

"'Active criminal investigation' means an officer has documented reasonable suspicion that a crime is being or has been committed, and believes the suspected criminal activity may be connected to a vehicle, a registered owner of a vehicle, or an occupant of a vehicle."

— § 41-6a-2002(1)

The data may not be used or shared for anything else

"Captured plate data obtained for the purposes described in Section 41-6a-2003... may not be used or shared for any purpose other than the purposes described in Section 41-6a-2003."

— § 41-6a-2004(1)(b)

"A governmental entity that is authorized to use an automatic license plate reader system under this part may not share or use captured plate data for a purpose not authorized under Subsection 41-6a-2003(2)."

— § 41-6a-2004(2)(b)

Every search must be recorded, and kept for five years

"(a) A law enforcement agency shall preserve a record of:
(i) the number of times a search of captured license plate data is conducted by the agency or the agency's employees or agents; and
(ii) the crime type and incident number associated with each search of captured license plate data.
(b) A law enforcement agency shall preserve a record identified in Subsection (6)(a) for at least five years."

— § 41-6a-2005(6)

Using these systems at all requires a policy that covers auditing

"A law enforcement agency may not use an automatic license plate reader system unless... the law enforcement agency has a written policy regarding the use, management, and auditing of the automatic license plate reader system."

— § 41-6a-2003(3)(a)

Misuse is a crime

"A person who knowingly or intentionally uses, obtains, or discloses captured license plate data in violation of this part is guilty of a class B misdemeanor."

— § 41-6a-2006

What the Records Show

Asked in writing for any audit, compliance review, or self-assessment of the system, the Weber County Sheriff's Office responded on August 6, 2026:

"This is not a record that we maintain."

Weber County Sheriff's Office, August 6, 2026

Network access is granted per agency, not per search. Once an agency is approved, its officers query directly, and no one at the county reviews the searches.

There is no mechanism available to Utah law enforcement to verify whether other agencies are following Utah's license plate reader law.

Meaningful accountability would mean auditing 3,343 separate agencies across 45 states. No authority in Utah has the power or the tools to do that.

The Core Finding

2,141,406 searches, 41.4% of the total, carry a stated reason that matches no purpose Utah law permits.

Every improvement in this record tracks a vendor product release rather than a county policy change. A case-number field first appears in February 2025. A structured reason menu rolled out in mid-2025.

What the County Actually Owns

A subscription. Not much else.

ItemDoes the county own it?
The cameras No. Title never passes to the county, and it may not remove, reposition, or take possession of them.
The images No. The contract excludes raw footage from what the county owns.
Anything derived from the images No. All derivative outputs, analyses, and models belong to the vendor, and the county has no right to export them.
A deletion guarantee The 30-day retention limit applies only to the data the county does own. Nothing commits the vendor to deleting the footage or the derivatives.

Accounts belonging to the vendor itself ran 179 searches of Weber County's data, including 78 by an account named Flock Safety - Sales.

Surveillance as a Service

Local agencies are not building surveillance systems. They are subscribing to them. The capability is rented, the hardware is rented, and what a county buys for its annual fee is a seat in a national network it does not control.

This Is Not a Weber County Story

Weber County released the records. It granted a fee waiver, answered eleven categories of request, and put an official on the phone with us. It is the only agency in Utah that has let anyone look.

The settings that produced this network are standard product features, documented in the vendor's own training materials obtained by the ACLU of Massachusetts in October 2025.

Every finding on this page is a question that should be asked of every agency in the state.

What We Are Not Claiming

  • Not that any of these searches was unlawful. Utah requires a record of the crime type and incident number, but does not require it to live inside the vendor's software. An officer who typed inv may hold a full investigative file elsewhere. What the records establish is that for 41.4% of searches, no authorized purpose is documented in any record available to the agency that shared the data.
  • Not that out-of-state agencies broke Utah law. Whether Utah's statute reaches them is an open question.
  • Not that Weber County acted in bad faith.
  • Not that out-of-state agencies targeted Utah. Agencies running broad multi-network searches reach these cameras automatically. That is the design, and that is the point.

One caveat on the totals. The 3,343-agency and 45-state figures depend heavily on 2022 and 2023, which contributed 1,993 and 3,078 agencies before a cutoff in December 2023. After it, annual agency counts are 40, 227, and 245. The provenance of the earlier data has not been established. The 2026 figures, 245 agencies and 98.6% out-of-state, do not carry that uncertainty.

What We Are Asking For

A legislative audit

Utah's Office of the Legislative Auditor General can audit any political subdivision and any organization that receives public funds. That reaches both the agencies and the vendors.

A verification mechanism

An agency sharing residents' location data should be able to find out whether the recipients are following Utah law. Today it cannot.

Disclosure

Every Utah agency operating these cameras should publish its current network sharing configuration. It is one screen.

Legislative action

Three ALPR reform bills have failed since 2025. HB0468 passed the House 64 to 2 and died on the final day of the session.
